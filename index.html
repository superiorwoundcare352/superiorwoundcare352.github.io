Community Education

Wound care tips for patients, families & caregivers

Small daily habits make a big difference. Here are a few general guidelines to keep in mind.

When to call a wound specialist

  • A wound shows no improvement after about two weeks, or hasn’t healed within a month.
  • Increasing redness, warmth, swelling, pain, drainage or a bad odor.
  • Fever or chills along with a wound.
  • Any foot sore in someone living with diabetes, even a small one.
  • Skin that turns dark or starts breaking down over a bony area.

Pressure injury prevention basics

  • Change position often. A common guideline is at least every 2 hours in bed, and more often when sitting for long periods.
  • Check skin daily, especially heels, hips, tailbone, elbows and the back of the head.
  • Keep skin clean, dry and moisturized, and manage moisture from sweat or incontinence promptly.
  • Use pillows or cushions so bony areas don’t press together or on hard surfaces.
  • Good nutrition and hydration help keep skin strong.

Daily diabetic foot checks

  • Look at both feet every day, including the soles and between the toes. A mirror or a helper makes it easier.
  • Watch for cuts, blisters, redness, swelling, calluses or color changes.
  • Wash and dry feet gently, especially between the toes. Moisturize, but not between the toes.
  • Wear well-fitting shoes and socks, and avoid going barefoot, even at home.
  • Tell your provider right away about any foot sore that isn’t healing.

Protecting fragile skin

  • Moisturize daily to help keep aging skin from tearing.
  • Pad sharp furniture edges, bed rails and wheelchair parts.
  • Lift, don’t drag, when helping someone move or reposition.
  • Long sleeves and pants add a layer of protection.
  • Keep fingernails trimmed and remove jewelry that can catch on skin.

General information only, not medical advice. Every patient is different. Follow the guidance of your own healthcare provider and call your provider with questions about a specific wound. In a medical emergency, call 911.
